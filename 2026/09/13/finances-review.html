Finances (Aug 2026 Edition)

Sep 13, 2026

I took a look at my finances this weekend and decided to make some charts. Really, these are just the finances from my credit card spending, so they don’t include rent, tuition, or the things included in those (e.g., bus pass), as well as some other miscellaneous items (e-transfers). I buy pretty much everything else on my card though, so it is quite comprehensive.

How I keep track of finances

I keep track of my finances using an SQL database. I have a Python script that reads in credit card spending histories that I download from my bank account. From there, I can categorize transactions from different retailers into one of the following categories:

  • Interest
  • Dining
  • Grocery
  • Recreation (this includes going to events, Spotify subscriptions, and pretty much any form of entertainment)
  • Housing
  • School
  • Laundry
  • Phone
  • Inner (this is money moving between accounts)
  • Misc
  • Transport
  • Clothing
  • Health
  • Gear (this one I only introduced this year)

Now, this categorization isn’t perfect, as it’s per retailer. For example, even though I might go to Breka Bakery to buy bread (Grocery), I can also buy sandwiches there (Dining), but all transactions there are categorized as Grocery.

Anyway, putting everything into an SQL database has some cool perks. I can query for pretty much anything I can think of:

  • Average monthly spending on groceries per year?
  • Breakdown of all transactions in a specific category in a specific year?
  • Total money spent at Chipotle? (This one is higher than I care to admit)

I can pretty much query for anything. This also allows me to make some cool charts on my spending habits.

The charts

Alright, let’s get into the charts. As a reminder, this data isn’t perfect, but it should give a rough idea of what’s going on in my financial life. The data in these charts spans July 2024 to August 2026.

This is probably the most interesting chart. The Other Physical Items category includes Misc, Clothing, and Gear, due to these three being a little interchangeable. As seen, my grocery bills have been steadily increasing (6-8%/year), which is higher than the rate of grocery inflation (3-4%/year). This means I’m probably buying slightly nicer groceries than I was two years ago, which isn’t entirely surprising.

The dining category has fluctuated a bit, increasing this past year. This is most likely due to an increase in trips and social events I’ve gone to this year. Although, I’m surprised to see it’s only about $24 higher than it was in 2024—about 1–1.5 meals’ worth—even though I’ve felt like I’ve been going out to eat a lot more in 2026. Maybe the times I go out to eat are just more memorable now because I almost always go out with friends, versus in 2024 when I was mostly dining out alone.

Interestingly, my Recreation spending has gone down from the previous year, even though I’ve been going out a lot more. I think this can be accounted for by hiking/camping (something I did a lot this summer) mostly having expenses in other categories (Transport, Gear).

Finally, we come to Other Physical Items. This one is, uhhh, a little high this year due to me buying a lot of expensive camping and hiking gear. I should really look into shopping for more second-hand stuff from now on.

Next up are some charts analyzing my spending from last year, number one being my total dining expenditure. Chipotle makes up a quarter of this. I had a bit of a problem last year…

It seems like Chipotle spending peaked from March to August, but has since come down to reasonable levels. I had a reason for it to be high during this period, which maybe I’ll write about another time.

Moving on, most of my grocery money went to No Frills. With the amount I spent, optimally using the Tuesday 10% student cashback meant I could save $420 over the course of a year. Of course, I didn’t always shop on Tuesdays, but I think I’ve made at least a couple hundred dollars from being a points member.

Finally, that brings us to overall spending in 2025. Over half my spending was on groceries. About 70% was on food.

The other things

Rent for me is about $1,000/month, and tuition is about $3,500/semester. A full year of studying (including the summer semester) would therefore cost me ~$34,000. Interesting…
